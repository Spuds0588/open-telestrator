Share and comment on any sport — free, in your browser

Open Telestrator is a free, open-source telestrator and live commentary studio that runs entirely in your browser, on a desktop or on a phone. Draw over any sport as it happens, commentate over the top, and send viewers one link — no account, no capture card, no cost, and nothing to install.

A full commentary setup, without the setup

  • Any sport, any source

    Go live over a match already playing in a browser tab, your webcam, or a phone that scans a QR code and becomes a second angle.

  • Commentary that points

    Arrows, circles and freehand drawing land straight on the picture, and instant replay lets you show the same moment twice.

  • One link to watch

    Viewers open a link and are watching straight away on any device. The stream fans out peer-to-peer, so a bigger crowd never costs a bigger uplink.

  • No cost, no kit, no account

    A webcam and a browser are enough. Open source, static-hosted and free forever — nothing to buy and nothing to sign up for.

From kick-off to viewers in three steps

  1. Pick a source. Share a browser tab that is already playing the action, or switch on your webcam.
  2. Commentate and draw. Talk over the play and mark up the moment as it happens; instant replay brings it straight back.
  3. Share the link. Send viewers the link or QR code, or embed the player on your own page.

One studio, every screen

The drawing, the cameras, the replays, the co-hosts and the viewer broadcast are the same on every device, because it is the same page. What differs is what the device underneath can do — and where it cannot do something, the control is left out rather than offered and then failed.

Questions, answered

Is Open Telestrator really free?

Yes. It is free and open source (MIT), with no account, no subscription and no paid tier. Broadcasting is peer-to-peer over WebRTC, so there are no video minutes to buy and nothing to meter.

What equipment do I need?

A computer with a modern browser and any camera you already own — a webcam is plenty. You can also commentate over a stream already playing in another browser tab, and phones join as extra camera angles by scanning a QR code. No capture card, mixer or studio is required.

Which sports can I telestrate?

Any of them. The video source is a camera or a browser tab, so it suits football, basketball, cricket, motorsport, grassroots streams and analysis nights alike. Draw over live play, or over a replay to show the same moment twice.

How do viewers watch the broadcast?

Send them the viewer link or put the QR code on screen. Viewers open it in any browser — phone, tablet or desktop — and watch live with play/pause, volume, full screen and picture-in-picture. One iframe embeds the same player in your own site.

Do I need an account or an install?

No account, and nothing has to be installed. Open the studio in a browser and you are live in minutes — on a desktop or a phone, and it installs as a PWA if you want it on a home screen.

How do I stream to YouTube or Twitch?

Over WebRTC, with nothing installed. A browser cannot open an RTMP connection, so the program goes to a service that accepts WebRTC and forwards it on. Restream is the easiest: connect your YouTube or Twitch account there, copy the WHIP URL from your stream's setup, paste it into the Broadcast panel and press Stream out. Cloudflare Stream and LiveKit do the same job if you would rather own the feed or run the egress yourself.

Can I run it on a phone or tablet?

Yes — open the studio on the phone itself. The layout moves to a bar along the bottom, every control is sized for a fingertip, the canvas takes a stylus and a hand resting on the glass is ignored. A phone cannot capture its own screen, so that one control is not drawn, and nothing else is missing. Add it to the home screen and it opens like an app.

Is the code open source?

Yes — the source is on GitHub under the MIT licence, and the whole studio builds to static files you can host anywhere, including GitHub Pages.
